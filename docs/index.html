Blind Box Pricing Model

Why is the hot character priced at 142? Drag the parameters and see what the model says.

1. Price consistency: expected value of confirmed figures vs blind box price

6 regular figures plus 1 hidden figure (335). Blind box price 108.

2. Inferring price sensitivity: revenue maximisation gives P* = 1/b

Demand D = a·e−bP, revenue R = P·D, maximised at P = 1/b. Observed price implies b. Curves are normalised to a peak of 1.

Assumes the seller prices optimally and the item sells. Cold-figure buyers are more price sensitive; fans of the hot figure are willing to pay more.

Model price > current price: room to raise the price (inventory-constrained or clearance-price view). Model price < current price: current price looks high. Each figure is iterated independently on its first-order condition; strict multi-product monopoly pricing under logit is a uniform markup, which can serve as a reference bound.

4. Dynamic inventory: hot figures rise, cold figures drift down

Recomputed daily with Q units left and T days remaining. Price P = ln(aT/Q)/b sells out exactly by the end of the horizon: tight stock (Q < T·a/e) gives a price above 1/b that rises as stock falls; overstock gives a price below 1/b that steps down, never below salvage value. 2 hot and 4 cold figures; the full-set bundle needs all 6 in stock.

Hot (price / stock)Cold (price / stock)

b is 1/142 for hot and 1/86 for cold (from section 2). As hot stock runs out the price jumps; if hot figures sell out first the bundle is delisted and cold figures can only be sold singly, drifting down further. The upstream decision (whether to open another box) depends on whether the expected marginal revenue of the next box exceeds 108.

5. Inferring seller profit: purchase discount to per-box profit by channel

Official price 129 per box (774 per set), seller price 108 per box, hidden figure 335 at probability 1/72. Cost c = 129 × purchase discount. Profit per box = revenue × (1 − platform fee) − shipping − c.

Break-even discount for opening boxes depends on both the discount and cold sell-through; for selling blind boxes at 108 it is (108 × (1 − fee) − shipping) / 129. When cold sell-through is below 100%, opening boxes to sell confirmed figures is worse than selling the boxes directly, which is why sellers open fewer boxes.

6. Finding the optimal price from a price test

Section 2 assumes the current price is optimal, which is circular. To test that assumption, you need independent demand evidence: sales at the current price and at a nearby test price. That gives the slope b = ln(D₀/D₁) / (P₁ − P₀). Daily profit is then π(P) = (P − c)·D₀·e−b(P−P₀), maximised at P = c + 1/b, where c is the unit cost, or the salvage value if the cost is sunk.
